Worked example, not a template

Three IMLS doors, all closing November 13. One of them a one-staff historical society should not walk through.

A funder fit sheet reads the actual notice against the actual organization: the gate, the scored lines, the match burden, and a verdict on each door. Below is one run end to end, quoted verbatim from the notices.

Subject org, used as the test case: 501(c)(3) historical society, one paid staff, owns its building, open 180 days a year, 6,000 object collection, no grant history. Every duty and figure below is from the funder's own document. Nothing about the org is guessed.

Door 1 of 3

Museums for America, MFA-FY27

Institute of Museum and Library Services · closes 11:59 p.m. ET, November 13, 2026 · request $5,000 to $350,000 · match 1:1, mandatory

The gate
"A private, nonprofit organization that has tax-exempt status under the Internal Revenue Code" plus a museum that "uses a professional staff", "owns or uses tangible objects", and "cares for these objects". All three criteria, all of them mandatory.
Scored on
  • "How well does the proposal align with the selected MFA program goal and associated objective?"
  • "Does this project advance the museum's strategic plan in specific and measurable ways?"
  • "How well has the applicant used relevant data and other evidence to describe the need, problem, or challenge"
  • "Are the project's intended results clearly articulated, realistic, meaningful, and linked to the need"
  • "Is the plan to sustain the benefits of the project beyond the conclusion of the period of performance reasonable and practical?"
Rejection first
IMLS rejects outright on "missing required or conditionally required application components", "submitted after the deadline", "requests funding outside of the applicable range", and a budget without 1:1 match on the IMLS Budget Form. Four doors close before a reviewer reads a word.
Verdict: go, at $20,000 to $30,000, collections stewardshipOne staff and a 6,000 object collection qualifies at 3(a) without argument. The match is the whole job: $25,000 of volunteer time and in-kind on donated equipment clears 1:1 and is countable, cash is not required. Small ask, tight project, one objective, measurable. This is the door.

Door 2 of 3

Museums Empowered, ME-FY27

Institute of Museum and Library Services · closes 11:59 p.m. ET, November 13, 2026 · request $5,000 to $350,000 · match 1:1, mandatory

The gate
Same three-part eligibility, and the project must be staff development. The program exists to "build staff skills", "grow their own professional development programs using apprenticeships and other alternative pathways", and "strengthen peer networks beyond a single institution".
Scored on
  • "Are the proposed activities informed by relevant theory and effective practice in professional development, organizational dynamics or change management?"
  • "Is the proposed project team structured in a way that is mutually beneficial to those involved?"
  • "Will the proposed activities generate organizational growth or change that result in a more agile and sustainable museum?"
Verdict: go only with a condition, $15,000 to $25,000One paid staff is thin for a staff development program on its own. It clears if the project carries volunteers and a partner institution, so the peer network is real and not claimed. Without a partner named in the application, this is the second door and the weaker one.

Door 3 of 3

21st Century Museum Professionals, 21MP-FY27

Institute of Museum and Library Services · closes 11:59 p.m. ET, November 13, 2026 · request $100,000 to $500,000 · match 1:1, mandatory

The gate
Wider applicant pool than the other two: museum, museum-field organization, or institution of higher education. Which is exactly why it is harder, not easier. Below $100,000 the application "may be rejected": the floor is $100,000.
Scored on
  • "Does the project address current needs of the museum workforce and have the potential to build career pathways and strengthen professional networks beyond a single institution?"
  • "Will the recruitment process employ effective strategies to reach the primary audience?"
  • "Are the time, financial, personnel, and other identified resources appropriate for the scope and scale of the project?"
Verdict: no-go this cycle$100,000 federal means $100,000 match and a workforce project with a recruitment pipeline. Eight days is not enough to build that on one staff member. Spending the week here instead of on door 1 is how a society ends up with three weak applications and no award.
